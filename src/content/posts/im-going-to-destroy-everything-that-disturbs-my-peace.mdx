There's a kind of exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix.

<EssaySoundtrack />

It's the exhaustion of explaining yourself to people who have already decided who you are.

Of being available for everyone while having nobody to call when you need something.

Of forgiving the same behaviour, accepting the same apologies, and pretending the same disappointments don't hurt anymore.

And then one day, something inside you changes.

Not dramatically.

You don't scream. You don't cry. You don't announce that you're done.

You simply sit there and realize:

**I cannot keep destroying myself to maintain peace with people who don't care about mine.**

And that's when everything changes.

## I've had enough.

Enough of conversations that leave me questioning my worth.

Enough of people who mistake my kindness for an invitation to disrespect me.

Enough of feeling guilty for saying no.

Enough of explaining my intentions to people who seem more interested in proving me wrong than understanding me.

And enough of making myself smaller so that other people can feel comfortable around me.

I used to believe that if I explained myself well enough, everyone would eventually understand.

If I was patient enough, people would change.

If I gave enough, someone would finally notice.

But I've learned something uncomfortable.

**You cannot make somebody value your presence by constantly proving how much you're willing to suffer for them.**

And I refuse to spend the rest of my life trying.

## Not everyone deserves access to you.

I want to be clear about something.

I'm not talking about hurting people.

I'm not talking about revenge.

I'm not talking about treating others like they're disposable simply because they disappoint me.

I'm talking about boundaries.

About knowing when a conversation has become an endless argument.

When a relationship has become a place where you no longer recognize yourself.

When an opportunity demands more of your dignity than you're willing to sacrifice.

And when staying somewhere begins to cost you the person you're trying to become.

You can care about somebody and still walk away.

You can forgive them and never invite them back.

You can wish them happiness without offering them another opportunity to make you miserable.

**Forgiveness does not require unlimited access. And love should never require the abandonment of self-respect.**

I wish I'd understood that earlier.

## But sometimes, the person disturbing your peace is you.

Here's the part nobody wants to hear.

Sometimes it's not the world.

Sometimes it's you.

Your need to be liked.

Your inability to accept rejection.

Your habit of reopening doors you already know lead nowhere.

Your obsession with what somebody might be thinking about you.

Your refusal to let go of the version of life you imagined.

Sometimes we blame people for disturbing a peace we've never learned to protect.

And sometimes what we call peace is simply our desire to avoid uncomfortable truths.

If someone tells me I've hurt them, I don't get to call that an attack on my peace.

If I make a mistake, taking responsibility isn't negativity.

If someone disagrees with me, that doesn't automatically make them toxic.

**Peace without accountability is just ego in a quiet room.**

And I don't want that kind of peace either.

I want the kind that allows me to sleep at night because I know I've tried to be honest, even when honesty was uncomfortable.

## I'm no longer available for everything.

I don't want to fight every battle.

I don't want to win every argument.

I don't want to explain every decision.

And I don't want to spend another evening mentally rehearsing conversations with people who aren't even in the room.

There are things I want to build.

People I want to love.

Places I want to see.

Books I haven't read.

Experiences I haven't lived.

And a version of myself I haven't met yet.

Why the hell would I sacrifice all of that just to remain available for somebody else's chaos?

I'm allowed to change.

I'm allowed to stop responding.

I'm allowed to choose a different direction.

And I'm allowed to disappoint people when their expectations require me to betray myself.

That doesn't make me selfish.

It makes me responsible for the one life I actually have.

## So yes, I'm going to destroy everything that disturbs my peace.

But not people.

Not relationships simply because they become difficult.

Not every uncomfortable conversation.

I'm going to destroy the habits that have kept me emotionally imprisoned.

The guilt I feel when I choose myself.

The need to be understood by everyone.

The belief that being kind means accepting anything.

The fear that losing someone automatically means I've lost something good.

And the version of me that keeps asking for permission to live.

I'm not interested in becoming cold.

I don't want to lose my compassion.

I don't want to stop loving people simply because some have disappointed me.

I want to remain gentle.

But I also want to become someone who knows when gentleness needs a boundary.

Because I've spent enough time being afraid of losing people.

**Now I'm more afraid of losing myself while trying to keep them.**

And perhaps that's the real meaning of protecting your peace.

Not building walls so high nobody can reach you.

But building a life where people can enter without being allowed to destroy everything inside.

I don't need revenge.

I don't need the last word.

I don't need anybody to regret losing me.

I just want to wake up one morning, look at my life, and know that it finally belongs to me.

**I'm not going to destroy the world to find my peace.**

**I'm going to stop destroying myself to keep the peace with the world.**

*Samir Seddiqi*

*14 November 2026*
