There is a particular kind of fear that only exists at the edge of something.

<EssaySoundtrack />

Imagine standing beside a lake.

The water is freezing. You know it. You've already touched it.

Someone beside you jumps.

You hear the splash. You watch them disappear beneath the surface and come back gasping for air.

And you?

You stand there.

You tell yourself you'll jump in a minute.

Maybe when the sun comes out.

Maybe when the wind stops.

Maybe after you've watched a few more people do it.

Five minutes pass.

Then ten.

And something strange happens.

**The water hasn't become any warmer. You've simply spent ten more minutes being afraid of it.**

I've been thinking about how much of life we live this way.

Standing at the edge.

Waiting for something outside of us to change so we won't have to feel what we're afraid of feeling.

And calling it patience.

## The beautiful excuses we make.

I think human beings are extraordinarily talented at making fear sound intelligent.

We rarely say, "I'm terrified."

We say, "The timing isn't right."

We say, "I need to think about it."

We say, "I'm waiting until I'm more financially stable."

We say, "I should probably gain more experience."

And sometimes those are perfectly reasonable things to say.

But sometimes they're just fear wearing a suit.

A beautifully dressed excuse.

Something respectable enough that nobody questions it, including ourselves.

I've done this.

I've convinced myself I needed more information when what I really needed was the courage to make a decision.

I've imagined every possible outcome until the possibility of actually doing something began to feel unreasonable.

And I've discovered something uncomfortable.

**You can become so good at preparing for life that you never actually participate in it.**

You research.

You plan.

You improve.

You wait.

And every time the opportunity approaches, you move the finish line.

Because preparation feels productive.

It gives you something to do without requiring you to risk anything.

You can spend an entire year preparing to have a conversation that would take five minutes.

And then congratulate yourself for being thoughtful.

What an extraordinary way to avoid living.

## The person you might never become.

Think about the things you've postponed.

Not the small things.

The ones that occasionally return to your mind when everything becomes quiet.

The person you wanted to tell how you felt.

The opportunity you didn't apply for.

The place you wanted to go.

The apology you never made.

The idea that keeps returning, no matter how many times you convince yourself it wouldn't work.

How long have you been waiting?

And what exactly are you waiting for?

Confidence?

Certainty?

Permission?

A guarantee that nothing will go wrong?

I wish somebody could give us those things.

I genuinely do.

Imagine how wonderful life would be if, before every important decision, somebody handed us a letter explaining exactly how everything would turn out.

You would know which relationships would last.

Which risks would pay off.

Which opportunities would change your life.

Which mistakes you'd regret.

But that's not how life works.

We make decisions with incomplete information.

We love people without knowing how the story ends.

We pursue things that may never happen.

And sometimes, despite our best efforts, we fail.

Not because we were foolish to try.

But because uncertainty is part of being alive.

**The future doesn't owe you reassurance before you enter it.**

And I wonder how many versions of ourselves we've already abandoned because we were waiting to feel ready.

## Waiting has a price, too.

We talk about the risks of action constantly.

What if you fail?

What if they reject you?

What if you embarrass yourself?

What if you lose money?

What if people laugh?

Fair questions.

But there's another question we don't ask nearly enough.

**What if you do nothing?**

What if you remain exactly where you are?

What if the person you wanted to know becomes someone you never speak to again?

What if the opportunity closes?

What if you wake up five years from now and realize that you're still waiting for the same permission?

We calculate the possible cost of making a decision.

But we rarely calculate the cost of refusing to make one.

Because inaction is deceptive.

It feels safe.

Nothing dramatic happens.

Nobody rejects you.

Nobody watches you fail.

You avoid the humiliation of trying something that doesn't work.

But time keeps moving.

People change.

Circumstances change.

And opportunities do not always wait for us to become comfortable.

There is something particularly cruel about regret.

Failure gives you an answer.

Regret can leave you with a question that never stops asking itself.

*What if I had tried?*

And perhaps the hardest part is knowing that, sometimes, you'll never find out.

Not because the answer was impossible to discover.

But because you never gave life the chance to answer.

## But don't mistake falling for flying.

Now I need to challenge this entire argument.

Because there's a particularly stupid kind of motivational advice that tells people to jump without ever asking what's underneath them.

Quit your job.

Risk everything.

Confess your feelings.

Invest everything you own.

Trust the universe.

What's the worst that could happen?

Quite a lot, actually.

Some decisions can destroy lives.

Some relationships are dangerous.

Some financial risks can leave people without the necessities they depend on.

And some waters are cold enough, deep enough, or dangerous enough that jumping into them without preparation would be an act of recklessness, not courage.

I don't believe being brave means ignoring consequences.

And I don't believe every opportunity deserves to be taken.

Sometimes waiting is wisdom.

Sometimes preparation saves you.

Sometimes walking away is the most courageous decision available.

But here is the distinction I keep returning to.

**Preparation should make you more capable of acting. Avoidance simply makes you more comfortable with not acting.**

Learn to swim.

Check the depth.

Understand the consequences.

Make sure the decision is yours.

But once you've done what you reasonably can, recognize when another month of thinking is no longer improving your judgment.

It's simply protecting you from discomfort.

Not every fall is freedom.

And not every refusal to jump is cowardice.

The difficult part is learning to recognize which one you're choosing.

## The tragedy of almost.

I think there's something deeply sad about the word *almost*.

Almost told her.

Almost started.

Almost left.

Almost applied.

Almost apologized.

Almost became.

An entire life can be built from things we almost did.

And from the outside, it might look perfectly respectable.

You could have a good job.

A comfortable home.

An impressive education.

People might admire how responsible you are.

Nobody would necessarily know how many dreams you've quietly buried.

Because we don't hold funerals for the lives we never attempted.

We simply stop talking about them.

We tell ourselves we've matured.

That our priorities have changed.

That some things weren't meant to happen.

And perhaps that's true for some of them.

But for others?

I wonder whether we've simply become afraid of admitting how badly we still want them.

Because wanting something gives it the power to disappoint you.

And if you never try, you can preserve the fantasy that you might have succeeded.

**Sometimes we protect our potential so carefully that we never allow it to become anything real.**

We would rather remain somebody who *could have* than risk discovering what we actually can do.

What a heartbreaking bargain.

## One day, there will be no later.

There is a reason this subject bothers me.

Time.

Not success.

Not ambition.

Not the opinions of other people.

Time.

Because I can recover from embarrassment.

I can learn from mistakes.

I can rebuild after failure.

I can apologize.

I can change direction.

But I cannot recover a year I never lived.

I cannot return to an ordinary afternoon with somebody who is no longer here.

I cannot go back to a moment when I had the opportunity to say something important and chose silence instead.

And I cannot keep assuming that the things I care about will remain available forever.

We live as though tomorrow is a personal entitlement.

As though every unfinished conversation will patiently wait for us.

As though the people we love will always be reachable.

As though there will be a perfect season when our responsibilities disappear, our confidence becomes unshakable, and life finally gives us permission to begin.

Maybe that season will come.

But maybe it won't.

And I don't want to spend my life waiting for a version of the future that exists only to protect me from the present.

I want to make decisions I can respect, even when I cannot guarantee their outcomes.

I want to tell people when they matter to me.

I want to attempt things that might fail.

And I want to become someone who understands the difference between being careful and being afraid.

Not perfectly.

Not every time.

But more often than I do today.

## The water was never waiting for you.

I want you to return to the lake.

You're still standing at the edge.

The wind is cold.

Your heart is beating a little faster.

The people who jumped before you have already stopped thinking about whether they should.

They're swimming.

And you're still negotiating with yourself.

Perhaps you're waiting for the fear to disappear.

But what if it doesn't?

What if confidence isn't something you're supposed to possess before beginning?

What if sometimes it only arrives after you've discovered that you can survive the discomfort?

Not because the water has changed.

But because you have.

And what if the most important decision isn't whether you're guaranteed to succeed?

What if it's whether the thing you're avoiding matters enough that you're willing to meet it without a guarantee?

I don't want to reach the end of my life and discover that I spent most of it waiting for conditions that were never going to exist.

I don't want to look back at all the things I cared about and realize I was more committed to avoiding embarrassment than experiencing life.

And I certainly don't want fear to have made every important decision for me while allowing me to believe that I was the one in control.

So if there's something you've been postponing, ask yourself what you're actually waiting for.

If the answer is preparation, prepare.

If the answer is safety, protect yourself.

If the answer is more information, find it.

But if the only answer left is that you wish it didn't feel so frightening, perhaps you already know what you need to do.

Because life isn't asking you to become fearless.

It is asking whether you're willing to live while fear is still present.

**The water doesn't get warmer if you jump late.**

**And the life you're postponing doesn't become longer because you were afraid to begin.**

*Samir Seddiqi*

*10 November 2026*
